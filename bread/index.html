Dough around filling

Nearly every cuisine wraps something in dough: dumplings, pies, buns, pastries, sandwiches. The fillings get the attention, but the wrapper is where the real variety is. Each point below is one dish, placed by three numbers from its outer layer: how wet the dough is, how much fat it carries, and how much yeast it uses.       What the axes mean

      All three are baker's percentages: grams per 100 g of flour (or masa, rice flour, starch).

      Hydration is all the liquid in the dough: water, broth, milk, and the water carried by eggs and butter. Stiff samosa and pie doughs sit near 30%. Dumpling and noodle doughs around 45–50%. Bread doughs 60–80%. Corn masa and scalded starch doughs go past 100%.

      Fat is the butter, lard, suet, oil or ghee worked into or laminated through the dough. Lean breads and dumplings sit near zero. Shortcrusts are around 50–65%, and puff pastry near 85%. Fat brushed between filo sheets after rolling is not counted.

      Yeast is given as instant-yeast equivalent. Chemically leavened doughs (baking powder or soda), steam-lifted laminated pastries and unleavened doughs all sit at zero; colour by Leavening to tell them apart.

      The numbers come from typical traditional and home recipes, rounded, so read each point as "roughly here". A little jitter is added in 3D so dishes with identical recipes don't hide each other. The table shows the exact values.
